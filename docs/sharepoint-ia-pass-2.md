## Content and findability

Small library definitions specify their own columns, optional content types, metadata and two to four views. Supplier context differs from technical decisions, workforce planning, research, production and financial guidance. New content types expose a relevant template cue and properties; ordinary Document remains available. Details/Edit show the same library-specific fields. Views filter/sort the same canonical files, and search includes folder context, selected metadata and content types.

Functional sources are enriched once, with canonical IDs, library membership and shallow folder paths. Teams Shared selects the actual SharePoint library and uses its definition. Specialised Audience, Content, Finance and Executive mappings are shared between SharePoint and Teams. Home/recent documents do not manufacture copies.

Current work has related file sets with varied dates/counts. Current productions are grouped by production rather than splitting each brief/draft/review into a separate folder. Research journey models, methods/read-outs and recommendation handoffs are similarly aggregated. Small historical/reference and genuinely focused review folders remain small. Selected documents have explicit dated version histories, including major/minor releases; duplicate v1/v2/final filenames are not used to simulate evolution.

See [the executable content audit](content-audit.md) for per-library populations and deliberately sparse cases. Count bands are prototype review heuristics, not ISO rules.

Homes now surface specific current work and tasks rather than repeating all left-navigation links. Logistics keeps its coordination entry; Digital highlights three active Teams channels. Other sites do not acquire automatic Teams panels.

Protected employee, candidate, customer, legal, technology, financial and archive areas remain boundary simulations with no added sensitive records. The Information Management Specialist's Teams administration exposes channel structure/settings, not protected record payloads.

## Research basis

Microsoft's [information architecture introduction](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/information-architecture-modern-experience) and [principles](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/information-architecture-principles) informed the purposeful navigation, library boundaries and shallow folders. [Libraries](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/libraries/introduction-to-libraries), [modern versus classic lists/libraries](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/data-and-lists/differences-between-modern-and-classic-experiences-for-lists-and-libraries), [content types](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/libraries/introduction-to-content-types-and-content-type-publishing), [creating/editing types](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/libraries/create-and-edit-content-types), [column options](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/lists/data-and-lists/list-and-library-column-types-and-options), [views](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/libraries/create-a-custom-view-of-a-document-library), [file information](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/libraries/view-and-edit-information-about-a-file-folder-or-link-in-a-document-library), and [content planning](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/sharepoint/sites-pages/plan-the-content-for-your-sharepoint-site) informed the prototype behaviours. Legacy Designer instructions in older support articles were used only for concepts, not as modern implementation steps.

The public ISO abstracts for [15489-1:2016](https://www.iso.org/standard/62542.html), [23081-1:2017](https://www.iso.org/standard/73172.html) and [23081-2:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/81600.html) informed business context, aggregation, metadata and record lifecycle distinctions. Full paid standards were not accessed; no ISO conformance or prescribed file counts are claimed.
